# Chapter 2-13 – Understanding Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVEs) for Security Analysts

**Status:** Completed  
**Focus:** Limitations of ChatGPT in providing up-to-date or complete CVE information

---

## Summary
This video demonstrates that ChatGPT is limited in its ability to provide information on specific CVEs, especially recent or high-profile vulnerabilities. The instructor tests multiple CVEs from 2020 and 2021 and observes that the model either lacks knowledge of them or cannot provide details. This highlights that while ChatGPT can assist in general cybersecurity concepts, it cannot be relied upon as a real-time CVE database.

---

## Key Takeaways

**Limitations**
- ChatGPT’s knowledge cutoff prevents it from providing information on CVEs released after its last update.  
- Even well-known, significant CVEs (e.g., SolarGate, Log4J) may not be present in the model’s database.  
- Asking for the “latest CVE” or recent vulnerabilities results in no useful information.  

**Implications for SOC Analysts**
- ChatGPT cannot replace authoritative vulnerability databases (e.g., NVD, MITRE CVE database).  
- Analysts must rely on official sources to track CVEs for patching, risk assessment, and incident response.  
- Use ChatGPT only for conceptual explanations of vulnerabilities, CVE terminology, or historical patterns that fall within the model’s knowledge cutoff.

---

## Notes for SOC Analysts
- Always verify CVE details with official sources before taking action.  
- Consider integrating automated feeds from MITRE, NVD, or vendor advisories for up-to-date CVE tracking.  
- ChatGPT can help explain the impact of a known CVE, but cannot be relied on to identify or describe newly disclosed vulnerabilities.
