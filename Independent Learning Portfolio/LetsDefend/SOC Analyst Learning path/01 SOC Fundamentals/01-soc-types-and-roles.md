# SOC Types and Roles

## Objective
Provide an overview of what a Security Operations Center (SOC) is, the different SOC operating models, and the key roles involved in SOC operations.

---

## What Is a SOC?
A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a centralized function where security teams continuously monitor, analyze, and respond to cybersecurity threats affecting an organization.

The SOC operates using a combination of **people, processes, and technology** to detect and mitigate security incidents.

---

## SOC Operating Models

Organizations implement different SOC models based on security needs, scale, and budget.

### In-House SOC
- Fully built and operated by the organization
- Requires significant budget and staffing
- Provides full control over security operations

### Virtual SOC
- Operates without a fixed physical location
- Analysts work remotely across multiple regions
- Often used to reduce infrastructure costs

### Co-Managed SOC
- Internal SOC team works alongside a Managed Security Service Provider (MSSP)
- Requires strong coordination and communication
- Balances internal knowledge with external expertise

### Command SOC
- Central SOC that oversees multiple smaller SOCs
- Common in large enterprises, telecom providers, and government or defense organizations
- Focuses on coordination and oversight

---

## People, Process, and Technology

A successful SOC depends on strong alignment between these three pillars.

### People
- Skilled analysts familiar with alerts and attack techniques
- Ability to adapt to evolving threats
- Willingness to research and learn new attack methods

### Processes
- Standardized workflows and procedures
- Alignment with frameworks and regulations (e.g., NIST, PCI, HIPAA)
- Ensures consistency and reduces missed steps during incidents

### Technology
- Tools for detection, prevention, analysis, and response
- Must align with organizational needs and budget
- Best-in-market tools are not always the best fit for every SOC

---

## SOC Roles

### SOC Analyst
- Typically divided into Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3
- Monitors alerts, investigates incidents, and recommends remediation actions

### Incident Responder
- Performs initial assessment of security incidents
- Focuses on identifying and containing threats

### Threat Hunter
- Proactively searches for hidden or advanced threats
- Investigates anomalies that bypass automated detection
- Focuses on identifying APTs and emerging attack techniques

### Security Engineer
- Maintains and integrates SOC technologies
- Builds and manages connections between tools (e.g., SIEM and SOAR)
- Ensures SOC infrastructure operates effectively

### SOC Manager
- Oversees SOC operations and strategy
- Manages staffing, budgeting, and coordination
- Focuses on operational efficiency rather than hands-on technical work

---

## Key Takeaways
- SOCs can be structured in multiple ways depending on organizational needs
- Effective SOC operations rely on people, processes, and technology working together
- Clear role separation improves detection, investigation, and response efficiency
